On February 9, 2011, the price of one bitcoin reached $1 for the first time on the Mt. Gox exchange. This milestone marked Bitcoin's transition from a sub-cent experiment to a currency with tangible monetary value. At this point, the total market capitalization of all bitcoin in circulation exceeded $10 million for the first time.

## The Journey to $1

Bitcoin had traded at fractions of a cent throughout 2010. The earliest known transaction valued bitcoin at approximately $0.0008 per coin when Laszlo Hanyecz bought two pizzas for 10,000 BTC in May 2010. By October 2010, the price had risen to $0.10 as awareness grew and the Mt. Gox exchange began operating. The final climb from $0.50 to $1 took approximately one month, driven by increasing media coverage and a growing user base.

## Significance

Reaching $1 was more than a psychological milestone. It meant that a single bitcoin could now be said to be "worth something" in conventional terms. Mainstream news outlets picked up the story, and Bitcoin was introduced to a much wider audience. The event triggered a surge of new users joining the network and contributed to a positive feedback loop of increasing demand and rising price.

At $1 per bitcoin, the entire supply of 21 million coins had a theoretical market capitalization of $21 million — a small but significant fraction of the global economy.

## Market Dynamics

The price movement was driven by several factors. The launch of Mt. Gox had created a reliable price discovery mechanism. Early adopters who had mined thousands of coins at negligible cost were beginning to sell small portions for fiat currency. New buyers, attracted by media coverage and word of mouth, entered the market. The supply of new coins from mining was also relatively high at 50 BTC per block, but demand was growing faster than new issuance.

## The Broader Context

February 2011 was also the month the Silk Road marketplace launched, which would drive significant demand for bitcoin as a medium of exchange. The combination of the $1 price milestone and Silk Road's emergence created a pivotal moment in Bitcoin's history: it was now simultaneously a speculative asset and a functional currency.

## Legacy

The $1 milestone is remembered as Bitcoin's first major price landmark. It demonstrated that a decentralized digital currency could command real economic value — a proposition that was still widely dismissed by economists and financial commentators at the time. Every subsequent price milestone (including $100, $1,000, $10,000, and $100,000) followed the same pattern of media attention, new user adoption, and price discovery that began with the climb to $1 in early 2011.
