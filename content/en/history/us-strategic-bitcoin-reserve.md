On March 6, 2025, President Donald Trump signed an executive order establishing the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and a separate United States Digital Asset Stockpile. The order directed the Secretary of the Treasury to create custodial accounts for all Bitcoin held by the Department of the Treasury that had been finally forfeited through criminal or civil asset forfeiture proceedings. Crucially, the order declared that Bitcoin deposited into the Reserve "shall not be sold" and would be maintained as a permanent national reserve asset.

![White House signing ceremony for the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve executive order.](media/history/us-strategic-bitcoin-reserve/white-house-signing-ceremony.webp "White House Strategic Bitcoin Reserve signing")

The executive order was the culmination of a campaign promise and a policy shift that had been building since the 2024 presidential election. The Reserve was capitalized with approximately 200,000 BTC already held by the U.S. government, primarily seized from operations against Silk Road, the Bitfinex hack, and other criminal cases. A separate Digital Asset Stockpile was created for other cryptocurrencies held by the government, though the order explicitly prohibited future acquisitions of non-Bitcoin digital assets outside of forfeiture proceedings.

The order framed Bitcoin as a uniquely strategic asset, calling it "the original cryptocurrency" and noting that its protocol "permanently caps the total supply at 21 million coins, and has never been hacked." It described Bitcoin as "digital gold" and argued that "there is a strategic advantage to being among the first nations to create a strategic bitcoin reserve." The Treasury and Commerce Secretaries were directed to develop budget-neutral strategies for acquiring additional Bitcoin.

The impact was immediate and far-reaching. The order signaled that the United States would not follow the path of China, which had banned cryptocurrencies, or of other nations that had treated Bitcoin primarily as a regulatory concern. Instead, it elevated Bitcoin to the status of a national reserve asset alongside gold and petroleum strategic reserves. The policy shift reverberated through financial markets, with Bitcoin's price reacting positively to the news. Several other nations and U.S. states began exploring their own Bitcoin reserve legislation, catalyzed by the federal government's endorsement of Bitcoin as a strategic asset. The order represented one of the most significant governmental validations of Bitcoin since its creation — a nation-state declaring, in formal policy, that the decentralized digital currency had become a matter of national strategic interest.
