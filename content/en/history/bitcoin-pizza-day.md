On May 22, 2010, Laszlo Hanyecz, a Florida-based programmer and early Bitcoin adopter, posted on the BitcoinTalk forum an offer to pay 10,000 bitcoin for two pizzas. He had been mining bitcoin on his GPU and wanted to test whether the digital currency could be used for real-world purchases. A 19-year-old in England named Jeremy "Jercos" Sturdivant took the offer, ordering two large pizzas from Papa John's and having them delivered to Hanyecz's home. The transaction was completed: 10,000 BTC changed hands for two pizzas worth about $41 at the time.

![Two pepperoni pizzas](media/history/bitcoin-pizza-day/pizza-day-pizzas.webp "Two large pizzas, the type Laszlo Hanyecz purchased with 10,000 BTC in 2010.")

## The BitcoinTalk Post

Hanyecz's original post on May 18, 2010, read simply: "I'll pay 10,000 bitcoins for a couple of pizzas.. like maybe 2 large ones so I have some left over for the next day." The thread generated discussion about whether bitcoin had actual value and whether this transaction would set a precedent. When the deal was completed on May 22, it established that bitcoin could function as a medium of exchange for goods and services — not just as an experimental token among cypherpunks.

## GPU Mining Pioneer

At the time of the pizza purchase, Hanyecz was already notable in the Bitcoin community for being the first person to mine bitcoin using a graphics processing unit (GPU) instead of a central processor. GPU mining was a significant leap in efficiency, and Hanyecz's custom OpenCL code gave him a substantial advantage. By the time he bought the pizzas, he had mined approximately 80,000 BTC. The 10,000 BTC represented about 12.5% of his holdings.

## Economic Perspective

The 10,000 BTC spent on two pizzas would be worth hundreds of millions of dollars at Bitcoin's peak prices. This fact is frequently cited in media coverage, but Hanyecz himself has stated that he does not regret the transaction. He has said the pizza purchase was a necessary step in proving bitcoin's utility as a currency, and that he considered it a successful experiment rather than a financial mistake.

## Cultural Legacy

May 22 is now celebrated annually as Bitcoin Pizza Day by the cryptocurrency community. It has become a symbol of Bitcoin's journey from a niche internet experiment to a global financial asset. The original BitcoinTalk thread remains online and is visited by thousands of people each year on the anniversary. The event is often referenced in discussions about Bitcoin's history, its volatility, and the importance of using cryptocurrency for real economic transactions rather than purely speculative holding.

Laszlo Hanyecz continues to be involved in the Bitcoin community and has spoken at conferences about his early experiences with mining and the famous pizza transaction.
