On July 9, 2016, at block height 420,000, the second Bitcoin halving occurred. The block subsidy dropped from 25 BTC to 12.5 BTC per block, continuing the programmed issuance reduction that occurs every 210,000 blocks (approximately every four years).
![Block 420,000 as displayed on a block explorer at the moment of the second Bitcoin halving.](media/history/second-halving/block-420000-explorer.webp "Block 420,000 — second halving")


At the time of the halving, Bitcoin was trading around $650, with a market capitalization of approximately $10 billion. Unlike the first halving in 2012, which occurred when Bitcoin was still relatively unknown, the second halving attracted significant media attention and analysis.

The event reinforced Bitcoin's predictable monetary policy. With the subsidy now at 12.5 BTC, the rate of new supply entering the market was cut in half, reducing annual inflation from approximately 8.5% to 4.2%. This steady reduction toward the 21 million cap remained a core feature differentiating Bitcoin from fiat currencies.

Mining difficulty adjusted in the following weeks, and the network continued to operate smoothly, demonstrating the robustness of the automated issuance schedule.
