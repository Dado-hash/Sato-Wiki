On July 17, 2010, Jed McCaleb launched Mt. Gox (an acronym for "Magic: The Gathering Online Exchange"), a website that would become the first dominant Bitcoin exchange. Originally built as a platform for trading cards from the Magic: The Gathering game, McCaleb repurposed the domain and code to create a marketplace where people could buy and sell bitcoin for US dollars. Within weeks, Mt. Gox became the primary venue for Bitcoin price discovery.

## The First Exchange

Before Mt. Gox, acquiring bitcoin was difficult. The only way to obtain the currency was to mine it yourself or find a willing seller through the BitcoinTalk forum, where transactions were informal and required significant trust. There was no established market price — trades happened at whatever rate two parties agreed upon. Mt. Gox solved this by creating a centralized order book where buyers and sellers could meet, with the exchange acting as the trusted intermediary.

The launch was modest. The first trade on Mt. Gox was for a few hundred bitcoins at fractions of a cent. But the exchange filled a critical gap in the Bitcoin ecosystem: it provided price transparency, liquidity, and a reliable mechanism for converting between bitcoin and fiat currency.

## Rapid Growth

Mt. Gox grew quickly as Bitcoin gained attention. By mid-2011, the exchange was handling over 70% of all Bitcoin trades worldwide. Its trading volume peaked at around 150,000 BTC per day during the 2013 bull run. The exchange's prominence made it the de facto reference price for Bitcoin across the entire cryptocurrency market.

## The Technology

The initial Mt. Gox platform was technically simple: a basic web interface connected to a Bitcoin wallet and a bank account. McCaleb wrote the initial code over a weekend. The exchange operated on a traditional order-matching engine: buy orders and sell orders were matched by price and time priority. Users deposited bitcoin or US dollars, placed orders, and withdrew their funds. Despite its simplicity, the platform enabled a level of market efficiency that had not existed before in the Bitcoin economy.

## Transition of Ownership

In March 2011, McCaleb sold Mt. Gox to Mark Karpelès, a French developer living in Japan. McCaleb had grown tired of the project and wanted to focus on other ventures (he would later go on to found Ripple and Stellar). Karpelès moved the company to Tokyo and incorporated it as Mt. Gox Co., Ltd. Under Karpelès, the exchange continued to grow but also began accumulating technical debt and security issues that would eventually lead to its collapse.

## Impact on Bitcoin

Mt. Gox's launch was a pivotal moment for Bitcoin. It transformed the cryptocurrency from a hobbyist experiment into an asset with a transparent market price. The exchange enabled the first wave of Bitcoin speculation and investment, attracting users who were unwilling to mine or negotiate forum trades. It also established the exchange model — centralized order books with fiat on-ramps — that virtually every subsequent cryptocurrency exchange would adopt.

The launch of Mt. Gox marked the beginning of Bitcoin's integration with the traditional financial system, for better and for worse.
