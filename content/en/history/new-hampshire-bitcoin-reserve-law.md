On May 6, 2025, New Hampshire Governor Kelly Ayotte signed into law a bill allowing the state to invest up to 5% of its available public funds in digital assets with a market capitalization exceeding $500 billion — a threshold that, at the time of signing, only Bitcoin met. The legislation made New Hampshire the first U.S. state to establish a framework for a strategic cryptocurrency reserve, beating a wave of similar efforts across the country.

![New Hampshire State House, where the crypto reserve bill was signed into law.](media/history/new-hampshire-bitcoin-reserve-law/new-hampshire-state-house.webp "New Hampshire State House")

The bill, sponsored by State Representative Keith Ammon, was the result of a coordinated campaign by the Satoshi Action Fund, a nonprofit advocacy group pushing state-level Bitcoin reserve legislation nationwide. Dennis Porter, founder of the Satoshi Action Fund, called the New Hampshire victory a critical breakthrough: "The first one's the hardest, by far. Having a state that's already gotten it done will really increase the political momentum."

New Hampshire's success stood in contrast to setbacks in other states. Arizona had been the first to get a similar measure to its governor's desk, but Governor Katie Hobbs vetoed the bill, calling cryptocurrency an "untested investment." Florida withdrew its reserve legislation. Other states including North Carolina and Utah saw varying degrees of progress, but none had crossed the finish line before New Hampshire. The law positioned the state as a trailblazer in public sector digital asset investment and created a template for other states to follow — a significant development in the broader story of Bitcoin's integration into governmental financial strategy.
